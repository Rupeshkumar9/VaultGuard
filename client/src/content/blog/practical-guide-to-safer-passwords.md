Strong credentials come down to **length**, **uniqueness**, and **isolation**. When every site has its own generated secret, a data breach on one service cannot cascade into your email, banking, or work accounts.

## Why uniqueness beats clever patterns

Attackers rarely guess passwords one by one anymore. Instead, leaked credential lists from older breaches are tested automatically across thousands of websites—a technique known as credential stuffing.

Even a seemingly complex password fails if you reuse it with small tweaks like adding `!2026` or the site name at the end.

## Generate instead of inventing

Use VaultGuard's built-in **Password Generator** directly inside the vault or browser extension whenever you create a new account or rotate an older login:

- Use **16–24 characters** by default for standard logins.
- Keep uppercase, lowercase, digits, and symbols enabled unless a legacy site restricts them.
- Save the generated credential immediately before submitting the form so you never lose access.

## Keep recovery notes organized

Beyond standard logins, store backup codes, card details, and secure notes in separate vault categories. Having recovery codes alongside your logins ensures you never get locked out when switching phones or resetting two-factor authentication.
